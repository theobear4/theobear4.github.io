little girl in the kitchen

there’s a little girl in the kitchen.
bent over the counter, i’m devouring
a piece of watermelon. the juice
is dripping down to my elbows, my shirt is
sticking to my skin. i want to take it off; the skin.
she’s staring at my back, loud as a hill.
i turn around and she sees me, like a deer
looking at a lion; red blood still on my chin.

she asks me for help, says she’s dying
of thirst and hunger. how can you be
hungry if you just ate? i just saw you.

like a vase of flowers her skull crashes
against the window pane. warm watermelon
juice pools on the white floor.

i’m killing myself and all i can do
is stand back and watch in horror.
i hold my warm body in my hands and hope
that it is still breathing and that it
never moves again.

maybe if i filled her guts up with cement
it would dry and she could sit up right and
i would heat her body up in the oven every
morning and people would think her blood
is still rushing though her veins.

august 2023

one of my first poems, written at 16 in greece.
